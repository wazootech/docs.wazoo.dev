## At a glance

|               | Worlds             | Document databases    |
| :------------ | :----------------- | :-------------------- |
| Data model    | RDF triples        | JSON-shaped documents |
| Schema        | Curated vocabulary | Flexible              |
| Retrieval     | Hybrid + SPARQL    | Index lookups         |
| Relationships | First-class        | Denormalized or joins |
| Provenance    | Fact-level ledger  | None                  |

## The philosophical difference

Document stores keep JSON-shaped records with flexible schemas and horizontal
scale. They fit records that change shape: profiles, events, and application
state, plus operational read/write patterns.

Worlds stores curated RDF facts in an append-only ledger. Retrieval is hybrid
(vector + full-text + RDF filters) plus SPARQL, and relationships between facts
are first-class. A document answers "what is stored"; a world answers "what is
true, and how do we know".

## When Worlds fits

- Facts must stay true and traceable, not just queryable.
- Agents need relationships between facts, not just flat records.
- You want verifiable answers through [SPARQL](/worlds/query), not index lookups
  alone.

## When Worlds does not fit

- Your records change shape constantly and a strict vocabulary is overhead.
- You need horizontal-scale document read/write as the primary interface.
- The document store is already the source of record and there is no
  agent-context layer.

## Coexistence

The honest split: document stores hold the source of record; Worlds holds the
distilled facts agents are allowed to act on. Keep source documents in your
document store and import distilled facts into a world through the
[import path](/worlds/update) when agents need to reason over them.
